Thirty lines after the chase

White Whale

  1. Call me the keeper of a lantern in the gale,
  2. where black waves heave like mountains seeking names.
  3. I saw the white brow rise beyond the foam,
  4. a moon of flesh against the thunder's jaw.
  5. The captain's eye burned hotter than the stars,
  6. and every rope sang doom beneath his hand.
  7. We chased not whale alone, but wrath made visible,
  8. a ghost with ribs of ivory and storm.
  9. O sea, old scripture written without mercy,
  10. your pages turned and drowned the prayers of men.
  11. The mast bent low as if it knew our sin,
  12. the harpoon flew, a silver curse uncoiled.
  13. Then silence split; the deep received our pride,
  14. and far off, pale and patient, rolled the whale.
  15. Only the water answered what we were.
  16. Beneath the keel, old kingdoms turned in sleep,
  17. their coral crowns untouched by human grief.
  18. The lanterns swung like planets out of law,
  19. and every face was carved from salt and fear.
  20. Ahab stood still, a sermon made of bone,
  21. preaching with silence to the listening storm.
  22. The whale rose white, not beast, but judgment's mask,
  23. and all the ocean gathered in its wake.
  24. Then ropes ran wild, and iron kissed the deep,
  25. while heaven shut its blue, indifferent eye.
  26. I clutched the rail and tasted blood and brine,
  27. a witness spared to carry ruin's name.
  28. For what is man who hunts his own dark wound,
  29. but driftwood dreaming it commands the tide?
  30. The sea remembers, and the sea remains.
